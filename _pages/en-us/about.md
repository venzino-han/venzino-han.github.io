I am an AI research engineer and a Ph.D. candidate at the **KAIST Graduate School of Data Science** (Knowledge System Lab), expected to graduate in February 2027.
My strength is **structuring complex problems and carrying them through to results**.
My research spans **LLMs, recommender systems, and the reliability and safety of AI agents**, with 29 papers (16 as first author) at venues including NeurIPS, EMNLP, ACL, CIKM, SIGIR, CVPR, and MICCAI.

Alongside research, I have led industry–academia projects that turn research ideas into systems that work on real data and in real environments — for example, making an on-device small language model agent call tools reliably for semiconductor inspection equipment.
Before graduate school, I worked in industry on data engineering, analytics-platform R&D, and data-driven market analysis at Buzzvil, Tmax A&C, and LG Hausys.

#### How I work {#how-i-work}

- **Design what to train and how to verify it.** Model performance is decided less by architecture than by what a model learns from and how it is validated, so I build data, sandboxes, benchmarks, and evaluation as one connected system.
- **Turn failures into testable hypotheses.** Instead of relying on intuition, I decompose the data, categorize failure cases, and trace whether a problem comes from data preparation or from training — then fix it there.
- **AI-native teamwork.** I split work into independently verifiable units with clear owners, and build tools that let teammates and clients inspect results directly, speeding up both development and communication.
- **Stamina and humor.** I sustain a high pace across long research and industry projects, and use humor to keep teams positive and candid even under pressure.
