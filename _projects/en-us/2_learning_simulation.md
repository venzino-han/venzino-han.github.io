**Role:** Project lead of a team centered on second-year undergraduate students — set the core technical direction and evaluation criteria, and gave fast feedback through code reviews and experiment results.

#### Challenge

Item analysis, student-behavior simulation, data generation, and evaluation all had to be built in a short time, with a small team that had little prior experience.

#### Approach

- Organized an **"AI-native" team** in which every member actively used AI to learn and execute quickly.
- Split the project into **independently verifiable units** — data construction, model development, experiments & evaluation — each owned by a member with a clear deliverable.
- Used LLM-based coding and research tools within a **requirements → implementation → test → verification** workflow, instead of taking AI-generated results at face value.
- Research outputs that existed only as data and logs were slow for both the team and the client to understand. I quickly built a **web-based visualization and verification tool** with AI tools, so that data, model outputs, student-simulation results, and evaluation results could be explored and compared directly.

#### Outcome

- The client could check results and give feedback on concrete cases without separate analysis requests, cutting verification time and repeated communication.
- Inside the team, failure cases and improvement directions could be discussed much more clearly.
- Members with little prior experience quickly became independent in development and experiments, and a small team built **LLM-based item-analysis and student-simulation pipelines**.

Good collaboration is not only about dividing the work well, but also about designing an environment where teammates and clients can quickly understand and verify results — and AI can speed up communication as much as development.
